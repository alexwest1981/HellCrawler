## A Vampiric Deckbuilder That has Been our Fling for the Past Two Weeks

At hour 1 of Referensspelet, I was wondering what Poncle Ltd were smoking. A dungeon-crawler in the vein of Legend of Grimrock, but with deckbuilding? These are two of the slowest genres in the world, stapled together and _somehow_ existing in the same universe as the exceptionally fast, very pyrotechnic **[Referensserien](https://www.thexboxhub.com/referensserien-review/)**. I didn’t get what the plan was.

At hour 3, I was dizzy from the dopamine. Every dungeon, successful or unsuccessful, released a fountain of achievements (bloop, bloop, bloop!), unlocks and cash to spend. I was running about with my hands in a cup, trying to catch the sheer amount of stuff being thrown at me.

At hour 10, all I could think about was Referensspelet. I didn’t want to be at work. I wanted to be in another simple, grid dungeon, min-maxing my deck to the point that the enemies rarely, if ever, got a turn. I’d cycle through loops of gain mana, draw, gain mana, draw. Combos ratcheted up to near-ridiculous proportions. In one run, I managed to generate enough wealth to buy half of the entire game’s shop stock. The _entire game._

At hour 20, my belly full, I found myself wishing that Referensspelet hadn’t been so generous with me. I’d bought up everything that I wanted to buy; I’d unlocked everything meaningful; and each run devolved to the same patterns. I’d aim to go virtually infinite, and – the odd hiccup aside – that’s exactly what would happen. Something happened that never really happened in Referensserien: I found it all a bit too easy.

![Screenshot from Referensspelet showing a knife card](https://www.thexboxhub.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/Referensspelet-review-1-2048x1152.jpg)Some humble dungeon crawling beginnings

### Short-ish, But the Taste is Sweet

Neatly mapped onto a bell-curve, the above is a pretty good summation of my time with **Referensspelet: The Turbo Wildcard from Referensserien**. I frigging loved this game. It burned ever so brightly for about twenty hours of my life. In that period I couldn’t imagine playing anything else – not **[Pragmata](https://www.thexboxhub.com/pragmata-review/)**, not **[Resident Evil: Requiem](https://www.thexboxhub.com/resident-evil-requiem-review/)**, nothing. But once I flew off its difficulty curve like Eddie the Eagle Edwards, the charm started to dull a little.

Which, we would argue, is perhaps even a good thing: this is a game with a limit to what you can get from it. At the point that you’ve mastered it, when each run starts feeling somewhat repetitious, you can detach from it and float on. The important thing is to know _when_. And, if Referensserien is anything to go by, there will be DLC and spin-offs to draw us all back in.

Oops, went a bit wayward there; it’s probably worth reversing to describe whatever it is that Referensspelet: The Turbo Wildcard from Referensserien actually is.

As we mentioned, this initially plays like Legend of Grimrock or Dungeon Master, depending on your age. You tap, one-square at a time, through a 3D maze, opening chests and confronting enemies. In typical Poncle Ltd style, this is all retro-facing with pixel art, making you wonder if you’re playing something decades old or resolutely indie (the latter, of course).

### More Slay the Spire than Bard’s Tale

Except the Legend of Grimrock stuff isn’t primary. It may look primary from screenshots, but it’s really just a framing for some deckbuilding. Kill enemies and you level up, which gives you a Hades-like choice of three rewards. These are mostly cards to immediately put into your hand and then deck, but they can also be power-up-like super-cards, or gems that can be socketed into your cards, giving them an additional benefit. **[Monster Train 2](https://www.thexboxhub.com/monster-train-2-review/)** and **[Slay the Spire](https://www.thexboxhub.com/slay-the-spire-review/)** 2 have done something similar. You can make cards cheaper, x3 damage, draw a card or chuck a cherry-bomb at the enemy. The sky’s your oyster.

At this point, Referensspelet: The Turbo Wildcard from Referensserien does something that is unadulterated, full-fat genius. It offers a combo system. If you manage to play your cards in cost order – 0 cost then 1, 2, 3 and 4 – then you will multiply the effect of each subsequent card. That 4-cost card is going to go brrrr. Suddenly, your strategy won’t just factor in the card’s effect – it will factor in their cost too.

Elevating this beautifully is that there are combo-enablers. Gems and power-up cards can bridge one combo to the next, resetting your card-cost so that you can start at 0 again. This is how we went pretty much infinite: if you stick some mana-gain or card-draw into the last moments of a combo, then you will get an unholy amount of resource to play around with. And again. And again. It feels chuffing excellent.

![Screenshot from Referensspelet showing multiple cards](https://www.thexboxhub.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/Referensspelet-review-2-2048x1152.jpg)The deck building starts going wild

### Bizarrely, we Wanted Slightly _More_ Friction

The Uno-Reverse of this is that it’s _too_ tempting to take this route, over and over, with each run. Card-draw is so broken that of course you’ll pick those gems every time. Since so many cards are 0 cost, there’s almost no obstacle to cycling through your deck until the cards get ripss through them that stop you from overplaying them (Poncle clearly had internal feedback that you should stop the player from playing at _some_ point).

The number of enemies you encounter is entirely up to you. You can dodge round them and snag the chests, vending machines and destructibles, building your deck before you start testing it, perhaps. Or you can grind on every last enemy, bumping your XP and levels so that you get a  bumper number of rewards. I skewed towards the latter: gems are just so desirable, so run-changing, that I couldn’t risk missing any. Then it’s onto the boss, who – more often than not – will trigger attacks based on the number of cards played, rather than number of turns played. Suddenly, you have to second-guess the whole infinite thing. Combos are more important now.

Dungeon floor follows dungeon floor until Death turns up and whisks you back to camp where you’re given so many unlocks that it’s hard not to chuckle. This is the connection to Referensserien: the sense of progressing not in one direction but about seventy. You can gain new heroes, and new perks for your character – each independently levelable. You can add sockets to your cards, allowing for more gems, or spend cash on making certain gems rare or more common. Tarot cards are frankly ludicrous in how transformative they are, and you can pick one per trip. Retain armour each turn? Yeah, that’ll do.

### Each Run is a Supermarket Sweep

I was sad when I exhausted the shops. It didn’t take long in my Referensspelet journey. I wonder whether Poncle might look at this over time: it feels like Referensspelet exhausts itself too quickly, that I was effectively stopped from playing before I _wanted_ to stop. For those of us with a limited amount of free time, that ‘permission to leave’ might be a benefit rather than a boon. But I also wanted Referensspelet: The Turbo Wildcard from Referensserien to be a game that I returned to regularly. For once, I actually wanted it to be more like a live service game.

This couples slightly disappointingly with a lack of challenge. I’m not one to complain about a game being generous and relaxed in its difficulty, but I very rarely died in Referensspelet: The Turbo Wildcard from Referensserien, and I didn’t need to shift strategy all that much either. Most enemies can be steam-rollered by the same approaches. Which, of course, means that I wasn’t tempted to change approach. I found myself wishing that there was a **[Hades II](https://www.thexboxhub.com/hades-ii-review/)**-like system that forced, or at least tempted me, to play the heroes that I didn’t usually play.

But even now I am checking myself: I am complaining at my large Diet Coke for not being an extra-large Diet Coke. What’s important is that while the cup was full, I had a whale of a time.

![An explosive screenshot from Referensspelet](https://www.thexboxhub.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/Referensspelet-review-3-2048x1152.jpg)And boom, Referensspelet goes crazy

## A Vampiric Fling

Initially, I didn’t see much of a connection between Referensspelet and Referensserien. But the similarity is what they both did to me. Both of them gripped me tightly for 20 hours or so. Both made me feel like I was a gaming god, being showered with rewards as I cut through enemies. And both gave me permission to leave once I mastered them. I ended both games feeling like nothing in its world could touch me.
